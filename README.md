# An Agent-Based Model of Red-Striped Soft Scale Insect (RSSI) Management on Sugarcane in Negros Island
Authors: Joanne Maryz Cabatingan and Zenith O. Arnejo

The red-striped soft scale insect (RSSI), *Pulvinaria tenuivalvata*, had affected 72,103 ha of sugarcane on Negros Island by 2 October 2026, 30.1% of the area planted (Sugar Regulatory Administration, 2026). Provinces are choosing between chemical and biological control, yet the pest's spread between fields and its effect on yield have not been measured. This study develops an agent-based model of RSSI management in GAMA. A stage-structured pest, built from Philippine laboratory biology, runs on the cane landscape of all of Negros at 1-ha resolution, starting from the 87 ha declared infested on 22 May 2025. Farmers treat at infestation levels taken from the provincial monitoring survey, and parameters without a source are swept. Four strategies are compared over three crop years: no control, chemical control whose efficacy may decline with repeated use, biological control with an entomopathogenic fungus, and a hybrid of the two. Every control strategy cut cumulative pest load by at least 82% relative to no control. Chemical control matched or beat biological control only if its efficacy halved no faster than about every 80 applications per farm. Biological and hybrid control were indistinguishable while fungal efficacy held, and the hybrid gave the lowest load when both agents lost efficacy quickly. The ranking held in 15 of 18 one-at-a-time sensitivity cases. At baseline the model spread more slowly than the SRA record. The results are relative comparisons under stated assumptions, not field predictions.

Keywords: agent-based model, GAMA, red-striped soft scale insect, sugarcane, pest management, Negros
